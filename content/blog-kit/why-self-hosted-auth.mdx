Of every part of a SaaS, authentication is the one you least want to rebuild after launch. It touches every request, holds your users, and quietly becomes load-bearing. So the choice of how to do it is worth making on purpose. This kit self-hosts auth with Auth.js, and here is why.

## Your users live in your database

With a hosted auth provider, your user table lives on someone else's servers, and your app asks them who is signed in. It is fast to set up and it works well, but it means the most important list you own, your customers, sits behind a third party's API and pricing.

Self-hosted auth flips that. Users are rows in your own PostgreSQL, next to their subscriptions and their data. A join, not an API call. When you want to know who your paying customers are, you query your own database.

## No per-seat tax on your own login

Hosted providers often price by monthly active users. That is a fee that grows exactly as your product succeeds, charged on the act of signing in, which you did not build. Self-hosting means the only cost of a user signing in is the database row they already have. Your success is not someone else's revenue line.

## You still get the real features

Owning auth does not mean a worse login. Auth.js gives you OAuth with Google and GitHub, passwordless magic links, email and password with proper reset, and account linking so a user who signed up with Google can add a password later. All of it running on your own infrastructure, all of it in this kit already.

## The honest tradeoffs

A hosted provider will hand you passkeys, enterprise SSO and advanced multi-factor flows faster than you can build them yourself, and there is real value in that if those are day-one requirements. Self-hosting means you own more surface area, including keeping it secure.

We made the call for ownership because auth is so expensive to change later. Starting with your users and your login in your own hands means you never have to run the migration you dread. If you would rather trade that for a managed provider's feature set, Auth.js can hand off to one, but the default here keeps you in control.
